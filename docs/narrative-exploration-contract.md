# Azure Mines — Narrative Exploration and Cartography Contract v0.1

**Status: agreed design direction (28 October 2026), not yet implemented.** This supersedes the older roadmap language that proposed a first-person grid exploration view. We are building a **choice-driven, authored narrative adventure** wrapped around the existing tactical combat engine.

## Core player experience

In exploration, the player reads an evocative **scene description** and chooses meaningful actions. This should evoke the pacing and consequence of gamebooks, but with persistent RPG state, a party of four, D&D skill and ability checks, branching conversations, quests, revisitable locations, combat transitions and an evolving journal/map.

An area might read:

> The room smells of wet stone and old smoke. A worried-looking goblin crouches beside a locked iron coffer. When he sees the party, he raises his empty hands. Somewhere beyond the southern door, metal scrapes against masonry.

Possible options:
- **Speak to the goblin.**
- **Examine the coffer.**
- **Search the walls and floor for hidden mechanisms.**
- **Open the southern door.**
- **Return to the entrance passage.**
- **Attack the goblin.**

These are **authored choices** with explicit requirements and outcomes, not generic verbs that automatically work everywhere. Some choices may invoke a die roll, offer further dialogue, reveal a map note, alter a quest, award a unique item, start combat, apply a condition, heal someone, change a location, or do nothing except deepen the fiction. A choice can have several consequences.

### Non-negotiable design principles

1. **Narrative leads; mechanics support it.** Descriptions, choices and consequences are written for the adventure, not autogenerated from tile movement.
2. **The same scene engine serves dungeons, wilderness, towns and conversation.** No separate fundamentally incompatible town UI or dialogue runtime.
3. **Revisiting matters.** An area can gain new descriptions, NPCs, objects, choices, dangers and secrets in response to flags, quests, time, NPC states and previous visits.
4. **No forced linear gamebook tree.** Areas connect as a directed *graph*, with loops, one-way routes, locked passages, conditional exits and optional discoveries.
5. **A location is not necessarily a tactical square.** Exploration areas are authored nodes; combat loads a distinct tile encounter with its own deployment, positioning and rules.
6. **Consequences persist.** If a trap is disabled, a chest opened, a goblin spared, a clue found or a quest accepted, re-entering the scene reflects that fact.
7. **Preserve the current dark, refined Gold Box-inspired interface.** Narrative text and old-paper cartography belong within the same colour, frame and typography system.

## Proposed user interface

- **Main scene pane:** title, flavour text, discovered objects and characters, relevant condition/status cues.
- **Choice list:** clear labelled actions, with disabled/conditional choices only when the player should know they exist. Hidden secrets stay hidden, not greyed out and spoiled.
- **Party strip:** four portraits, HP/conditions, resources and pooled gold, with optional character selection for checks or interactions.
- **Journal/context pane:** recent discoveries, dialogue history, quest journal and item descriptions; player can inspect quest objects and their provenance.
- **Map button or pane:** switch to an old-school, hand-annotated dungeon/region map, retaining location context while explored content remains visible.
- **Result presentation:** narrative outcome and rolled check details, plus terse mechanical deltas (e.g. *Pip: Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) 17 vs DC 15 — success; trap disarmed*). Do not hide dice/bonuses; when a roll could affect the story, make its stakes understandable where appropriate.
- **Combat handoff:** an authored choice or conditional scene event opens the established deployment + turn-based encounter, and the combat outcome routes back to an authored narrative location/variant.

Scene descriptions and choices should be sized for readability rather than cramped into the present combat-log sidebar. The story view is the main experience outside combat; the tactical map becomes central during battle.

## Data model and boundaries

**1. Location node**: unique ID, area/region, public title, variants of authored description, authored exit links, characters, objects, interactions, possible ambient event, map reference.

**2. Choice / interaction**: stable ID, displayed label, visibility condition, enabled condition (if any), action/check requirements, authored success/failure/other outcomes, next scene/interaction, and explicit effects. Multiple effects may be bundled atomically.

**3. World and party state**: scene/quest flags, visited nodes, seen/used interactions, unlocked routes, discovered secrets, persistent NPC state, inventory/currencies, party HP, level and per-character XP. All must serialize; content IDs must remain stable across saves.

**4. Checks**: Classic 5E SRD 5.2.1 ability checks, skill/tool proficiency, skill modifier, DC, Advantage/Disadvantage and legitimate party member choice. Only roll when consequences exist. A failed check may consume time/resources, add danger, introduce a new choice or close a route; it need not block the main story forever.

**5. Consequences**: whitelisted, typed operations, e.g. set_flag, reveal_choice, reveal_exit, grant_xp, grant_gold, grant_consumable, grant_named_item, heal, apply_condition, modify_reputation, advance_quest, start_combat, move_location, discover_map_note. No executable scripts embedded in adventure JSON. State and rewards are committed exactly once per unique consequence instance.

**6. Quest definition**: stages, objectives, optional paths, rewards, tracked evidence/quest objects, dialogue hooks and completion state. Journal text is authored. An object can be shown in a *quest-object inspector* with artwork, description, clues and history.

**7. Conversations**: dialogue is a reusable form of scene/choice graph, not just static NPC speech. Dialogue options may invoke Persuasion, Insight, Deception, Intimidation, attribute checks, reputation checks, quest checks, trades or combat. A merchant, captive goblin, town official and quest-giver all use the same interaction engine.

## Old-school dungeon and wilderness cartography

The map should resemble a carefully drawn **graph-paper dungeon map**, inked on aged paper with understated grid lines, irregular ink strokes and handwritten labels/note callouts. It is **not** an omniscient Google-style minimap, and it is not a substitute for choosing narrative exits.

- Every area has an authored **map node ID** and optional coordinates/shape; links encode corridors, paths, doors and stairs.
- **Reveal areas only through visits, discoveries, clues or special abilities.** Undiscovered regions remain blank/obscured; secret passages don't appear before discovery.
- Each explored area can reveal its outline and known exits. Some parts may be marked *unmapped*, *suspected* or *blocked*.
- Attach scribbled notes such as *"goblin tracks"*, *"sealed door"*, *"strange draught"* or *"trap here"*. Notes derive from verified party knowledge and authored discoveries, never inaccessible hidden content.
- Allow player-authored notes and labels later, clearly distinguished from game-authored annotations.
- Map coordinates are presentation data; **connections in the graph are authoritative**. Strange non-Euclidean or one-way links must remain representable.
- Towns can use the same idea at a district/building scale, with a different parchment style if appropriate.
- Map opening never silently moves the party, consumes movement or reveals secret locations.

**Art-production approach:** create an actual reusable grid-paper texture and parchment frame, plus pen-style doors, stairs, corridor and room marks. Render room/connection geometry and journal annotations **as separate layers** so player discovery state controls visibility. Do not bake hidden map secrets into a single always-visible background image.

## Checks and story fairness

- Specify who can attempt each check and whether the best party member, a selected character or a group check applies.
- Clearly expose DC and outcome when sensible; some mystery rolls may remain concealed by explicit author choice.
- Prevent repeated save/loading or clicking from rerolling a single attempted check unless its content explicitly permits retry with a cost, new approach or changed circumstances.
- Ensure that essential narrative progress never depends exclusively on one irreversible low-probability roll. Failure can lead to another route, harder combat, loss of supplies, delayed reward or different quest state.
- Keep random results seeded and serialized. Preserve the outcome of a once-only check across revisits.
- Temporary effects have clear durations/triggers. Narrative healing and combat damage must update the **same party state**.

## First integration slice — proposed v0.8

Use the existing wagon ambush as a bridge into *narrative exploration*, without building a full dungeon or town prematurely:

1. **Old Timber Road**: opening narrative, observe wagon/tracks, choose approach; authored transition to deployment and combat.
2. **Wagon Clearing (after the battle)**: inspect the wagon; search chests or ashes; optional Investigation or Perception check; grant a minor clue or a carefully limited consumable, with no equipment trash.
3. **Overgrown Track**: a discoverable, persistent side route, plus a return link to the wagon clearing with updated prose.
4. **Town Approach**: one original NPC encounter with branching dialogue and a simple quest hook; postpone a full merchant/shop system.
5. **Map**: reveal each visited location, connections and at least one handwritten label or discovered secret note.
6. **Save/load**: persist scene ID, party HP/resources, XP, gold, visited areas, interaction/check results, item awards, quest flags and map discovery. Reopening the game cannot reroll or reclaim resolved content.

### Acceptance criteria

- Choices are data-driven and can be gated by flags without hardcoding one decision tree into the scene UI.
- The scene description visibly changes when revisited after an event.
- One check uses the canonical character sheet, shows its mechanical resolution and records its outcome.
- A discovered path appears on both the choice list and the map only after discovery.
- A narrative choice can start the existing four-goblin tactical encounter; winning returns to the correct aftermath node.
- A town-like conversation uses the same interaction engine and can accept/update a quest.
- Save/reload reproduces the same map, NPC/quest state, pooled gold, party XP and one-time loot results.
- The map conveys a hand-drawn dungeon aesthetic while revealing **only discovered** locations/exits.

## Deferred

Real-time or first-person walking, random room generators, procedural prose as core content, fully-fledged dialog editor, massive quest catalogue, procedural town simulation, full journal UI, dynamic lighting and hand-drawn map animation. Expand only after this authored slice plays cleanly.

## Relationship to existing milestones

The new exploration framework should be the next **major engine system**, before extending the adventure into town shops or dungeon depth. It retains our existing **Classic 5E combat rules**, the low-magic loot philosophy and the styled party/combat UI, but **replaces first-person-grid exploration as a project goal**.
