# Location inspiration: Yorkshire Dales, Cumbria and the western coast

> **Research / inspiration bank — not established setting canon or an implementation commitment.**  
> **Compiled:** 13 November 2026 | **Version:** 1.0 | **Entries:** LOC-01–LOC-32  
> [Research index](README.md) · [Folklore companion](northern-folklore-and-old-powers.md) · [World bible](../world-bible.md)

## Purpose and provenance

Preserve the geographical, geological, architectural and industrial references discussed in the Project Azure Mines conversation on 13 November 2026, together with their proposed game applications. This is a structured consolidation, not a verbatim transcript. Source links are retained from that discussion; this documentation pass is not a fresh independent audit of every historical claim or live webpage.

**Real-world reference** describes the cited place. **Azure Mines possibilities** are original design suggestions, not claims about that place and not approved additions to the fictional world. Some places carry legends; those are identified as legends rather than archaeological evidence. Real-world names are research labels, not automatic in-game names.

The proposed regional approach is a **Yorkshire Dales core**, a more mountainous western mining district inspired by Cumbria, and a distinct coastal frontier drawing on the Cumbrian coast and Morecambe Bay. This is an artistic palette, not a proposal to copy the real map or relocate established settlements.

## Limestone landscapes and underground locations

<a id="loc-01"></a>
### LOC-01 — Gordale Scar

**Area:** Malhamdale, Yorkshire Dales. **Reference type:** Limestone gorge; waterfall; concealed reveal.

**Real-world reference:** A limestone gorge whose entrance conceals the scale of the cliffs and waterfalls beyond. An apparently modest valley can open into a much larger, more dramatic space. [Source: Yorkshire Dales National Park, Gordale Scar](https://www.yorkshiredales.org.uk/places/gordale_scar/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A concealed approach to a cave complex or stronghold. The party follows a stream between narrowing walls before discovering a waterfall, elevated entrance and defenders controlling the approach. Borrow the sequence of reveals rather than simply reproducing a photograph. Especially strong for the existing goblin hideout's wooded-valley approach.

<a id="loc-02"></a>
### LOC-02 — Malham Cove and limestone pavement

**Area:** Near Malham. **Reference type:** Limestone cliff; fractured upland terrain.

**Real-world reference:** A great curved limestone cliff topped by pavement composed of blocks and fissures, called clints and grikes. The upper surface has a markedly different character from the valley below. [Source: Yorkshire Dales National Park, Malham special qualities](https://www.yorkshiredales.org.uk/places-to-go/malham/malham-special-qualities/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A pale, fractured upland above an old settlement or sealed entrance. Cracks conceal small creatures, lost equipment and openings into lower passages. This offers distinctive outdoor terrain that is neither woodland nor ordinary scree, with navigational and footing problems as well as combat cover.

<a id="loc-03"></a>
### LOC-03 — Gaping Gill and Ingleborough Cave

**Area:** Ingleborough. **Reference type:** Surface-to-underground water network; vertical shaft.

**Real-world reference:** Fell Beck plunges roughly 100 metres into the enormous chamber of Gaping Gill; its drainage connects with Ingleborough Cave farther down the hillside. Surface geography and deep passages belong to the same water system. [Source: Yorkshire Dales National Park, Gaping Gill](https://www.yorkshiredales.org.uk/things-to-do/get-outdoors/adventure-activities/caving/gaping-gill/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A landmark chamber reached first through winding passages and later through a restored winch shaft. Falling water and daylight make it recognisable from different approaches. Recovering heavy treasure or an injured companion becomes an expedition problem requiring rope, equipment and a workable route, not merely victory in combat.

<a id="loc-04"></a>
### LOC-04 — Yordas Cave

**Area:** Kingsdale. **Reference type:** Entrance chamber; stream passages; waterfall chamber.

**Real-world reference:** A large entrance chamber connected to a stream, smaller passages and a separate waterfall chamber. Its arrangement is particularly relevant to the cave discussed for the opening adventure. [Source: Yorkshire Dales National Park, Yordas Cave](https://www.yorkshiredales.org.uk/places/yordas_cave/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** The strongest individual reference for the existing goblin cave's spatial and hydrological character. A central hall links inhabited side chambers while the waterfall explains the stream and supplies sound and atmosphere. Preserve the author's waterfall–pool–small-stream arrangement and a walkable main corridor; this reference does not authorise changing the agreed room roster or restoring superseded layouts.

<a id="loc-05"></a>
### LOC-05 — Ease Gill Caverns and the Three Counties System

**Area:** Western Dales border country, beneath Yorkshire, Lancashire and Cumbria. **Reference type:** Connected regional cave network.

**Real-world reference:** An extensive linked cave system with multiple entrances, active stream passages and older high-level routes, rather than isolated chambers. [Source: Yorkshire Dales National Park, Three Counties System](https://www.yorkshiredales.org.uk/things-to-do/get-outdoors/adventure-activities/caving/three-counties-system/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** Separate adventures gradually reveal that a mine, bandit refuge, spring cave and distant sinkhole are connected. Newly discovered exits change the overland location graph and create alternative routes between valleys. Connections should emerge through exploration rather than appear on the player's map before discovery.

<a id="loc-06"></a>
### LOC-06 — Troller's Gill

**Area:** Near Appletreewick. **Reference type:** Gloomy gorge; intermittent surface water; local monster tradition.

**Real-world reference:** A gorge whose stream usually runs underground, leaving a dry bed. Local folklore associates it with the Barguest, and mining remains occur nearby. The supernatural association is a tradition, not a verified animal record. [Source: Yorkshire Dales National Park, Troller Gill](https://www.yorkshiredales.org.uk/places/troller_gill/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A feared shortcut whose reputation could conceal smugglers, a genuine supernatural presence, or both. Weather changes the route from passable ground to a water hazard, creating a time-versus-risk decision. Pair with [FOL-09, the barguest](northern-folklore-and-old-powers.md#fol-09), without requiring every ominous gorge to contain the same creature.

<a id="loc-07"></a>
### LOC-07 — Norber Erratics

**Area:** Near Austwick. **Reference type:** Glacial boulders; apparently intentional stone placement.

**Real-world reference:** Glacially transported boulders include striking examples perched on limestone pedestals where the underlying rock has been protected from erosion. Their appearance can suggest deliberate placement despite their natural origin. [Source: Dales Rocks, glacial erratics](https://dalesrocks.org.uk/ribblesdale/geological-processes/glacial-erratics/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A giant's playing field, ancestral meeting place or landscape of supposed petrified creatures. An investigation begins when one supposedly immovable ancient boulder has recently shifted, exposing or hiding something. The natural explanation and the local story need not be identical.

## Water, high routes and mining landscapes

<a id="loc-08"></a>
### LOC-08 — Hardraw Force

**Area:** Wensleydale. **Reference type:** Single-drop waterfall; wooded ravine.

**Real-world reference:** A dramatic single-drop waterfall within a wooded ravine, its form shaped by the surrounding layered rocks. [Source: Hardraw Force, history](https://www.hardrawforce.com/history.html).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A secluded meeting place, shrine or refuge. The roar conceals conversation and movement; the approach supports tracking and ambush. Prefer a compact, memorable encounter here rather than automatically putting a large dungeon behind every waterfall.

<a id="loc-09"></a>
### LOC-09 — The Strid and Strid Wood

**Area:** Bolton Abbey estate, Wharfedale. **Reference type:** Narrow river channel; woodland; deceptive hazard.

**Real-world reference:** Ancient woodland surrounds a place where the Wharfe is forced into a narrow channel. Its small apparent width conceals a genuinely dangerous river feature. [Source: Bolton Abbey, Strid Wood and the Strid](https://boltonabbey.com/what-to-see/strid-wood-and-the-strid/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** An environmental obstacle that cannot sensibly be solved by fighting. A damaged bridge, absent ferryman or flooded diversion forces a choice between spending supplies, finding another crossing or taking a longer route. Communicate danger clearly; apparent visual jumpability must not become an unfair instant-death trap. Water-horse folklore offers a separate optional layer, not a necessary explanation of the hazard.

<a id="loc-10"></a>
### LOC-10 — Semer Water

**Area:** Raydale, off Wensleydale. **Reference type:** Lake; drowned-settlement legend; hospitality.

**Real-world reference:** A natural lake associated with stories of a settlement drowned after its inhabitants refused hospitality to a stranger. This is folklore, not evidence that a town lies beneath the water. [Source: Yorkshire Dales National Park, Semer Water](https://www.yorkshiredales.org.uk/places/semer_water/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** In the fictional adaptation, a settlement really could be submerged: rooftops exposed during drought, bells heard at night, or a causeway revealed only under particular conditions. The hospitality theme supports meaningful decisions about sharing scarce food rather than reducing the location to another monster hunt.

<a id="loc-11"></a>
### LOC-11 — Buttertubs Pass

**Area:** Between Wensleydale and Swaledale. **Reference type:** Exposed upland crossing; limestone potholes.

**Real-world reference:** A high moorland crossing with characteristic limestone potholes near the summit. A traditional explanation of the name has farmers lowering butter into the holes to keep it cool. [Source: Yorkshire Dales National Park, Buttertubs Pass](https://www.yorkshiredales.org.uk/places/buttertubs_pass/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A fictional exposed pack route while wagons must use a longer valley road. Mist, potholes and a damaged path make ropes, local knowledge and load management useful. This suggested transport restriction belongs to the fictional route; it is not a claim about vehicle access on the real modern pass.

<a id="loc-12"></a>
### LOC-12 — Cautley Spout

**Area:** Howgill Fells, near Sedbergh. **Reference type:** Waterfall; upland settlement remains.

**Real-world reference:** A conspicuous waterfall on a steep hillside, with remains of an Iron Age settlement in the valley below. [Source: Yorkshire Dales National Park, Cautley Spout](https://www.yorkshiredales.org.uk/places/cautley_spout/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A deserted upland community beneath a sacred cascade. Ruined enclosures, a sheltered camp and an old processional path provide a quieter archaeological encounter. Investigating why people left need not culminate in combat. The waterfall also gives the regional map a recognisable landmark.

<a id="loc-13"></a>
### LOC-13 — Gunnerside Gill

**Area:** Swaledale. **Reference type:** Mining landscape; ore processing; water power; hushes.

**Real-world reference:** Mine entrances, processing remains, waterwheel sites and scarred hillsides show an entire industrial landscape. Some scars are hushes: gullies excavated or enlarged through controlled releases of water to expose or work mineral veins. [Source: Yorkshire Dales National Park, lead-mining trails](https://www.yorkshiredales.org.uk/things-to-do/get-outdoors/lead-mining-in-the-dales/trails/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A leading reference for the wider mining district. Replace the isolated entrance-in-a-cliff cliché with abandoned levels, workshops, channels, spoil and competing routes. Different factions control separate parts of one industrial operation. Exploration can reveal how the district worked before revealing what brought it to crisis.

<a id="loc-14"></a>
### LOC-14 — Old Gang lead mines and smelt mill

**Area:** Swaledale. **Reference type:** Processing settlement; furnaces; fuel storage; surface infrastructure.

**Real-world reference:** A substantial mining complex with smelting ruins, a long peat store, dressing floors, mine levels and spoil heaps. Much of the mine's infrastructure existed outside the underground workings. [Source: Yorkshire Dales National Park Access Hub, Old Gang Smelt Mill](https://accesshub.yorkshiredales.org.uk/places/old-gang-smelt-mill-route/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A derelict dwarven processing settlement. The central mystery might concern a furnace, a fuel shortage or diverted water rather than the deepest tunnel. Restoring a workshop could establish a useful expedition outpost. The settlement should feel like a former workplace, not a dungeon designed solely for adventurers.

## Castles, religious houses and crossings

<a id="loc-15"></a>
### LOC-15 — Bolton Castle

**Area:** Wensleydale. **Reference type:** Courtyard castle; inhabited regional power centre.

**Real-world reference:** A late-medieval castle arranged around a rectangular courtyard, with substantial corner towers and interconnected ranges. Its compact plan contrasts with a sprawling fortress. [Source: Yorkshire Dales National Park, Bolton Castle — a home, a prison, a ruin](https://www.yorkshiredales.org.uk/bolton-castle-a-home-a-prison-a-ruin/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A strong model for an inhabited regional stronghold. Domestic, political and military spaces share a courtyard: audiences, servants, guards, prisoners and competing interests. It supports infiltration and social investigation without an implausibly vast floor plan. Do not automatically replace Vale Manor or assign this structure to an existing lord.

<a id="loc-16"></a>
### LOC-16 — Middleham Castle

**Area:** Lower Wensleydale fringe. **Reference type:** Massive keep; layered construction; military seat.

**Real-world reference:** An immense Norman keep surrounded by later ranges of residential chambers. The contrast between the heavy core and subsequent accommodation provides useful architectural reference. [Source: English Heritage, Middleham Castle](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/middleham-castle/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A formidable military seat whose oldest core predates its ruling family. Later building phases hide blocked doors, forgotten stores and disputed ownership symbols. Choose this reference when a stronghold should feel genuinely difficult to assault, not merely look impressive in a background image.

<a id="loc-17"></a>
### LOC-17 — Barden Tower

**Area:** Wharfedale. **Reference type:** Fortified residence; hunting forest administration.

**Real-world reference:** A ruined fortified residence associated with the management of a hunting forest, including use as a hunting lodge and principal residence. [Source: Bolton Abbey, Barden Tower](https://boltonabbey.com/what-to-see/barden-tower/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A secondary antagonist's headquarters, abandoned ranger station or disputed hunting lodge. Poaching, forest rights and missing tenants provide local stakes. Its modest scale avoids giving every local landholder an enormous castle. It remains an architectural reference, not a new decision about the canonical high-moor tower's builders or history.

<a id="loc-18"></a>
### LOC-18 — Bolton Priory

**Area:** Bolton Abbey, Wharfedale. **Reference type:** Riverside religious estate; supporting buildings.

**Real-world reference:** Remains of an Augustinian priory and evidence of its precinct and supporting buildings. Despite the familiar Bolton Abbey place-name, the religious foundation was a priory. [Source: Bolton Abbey, Priory Church and Ruins](https://boltonabbey.com/what-to-see/priory-church-and-ruins/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A former religious estate whose significance extends beyond the church: guest accommodation, stores, waterworks and boundaries show what it once controlled. It could be a sanctuary, contested inheritance or cautiously resettled site. Ruined religious architecture does not automatically imply an undead dungeon.

<a id="loc-19"></a>
### LOC-19 — Pendragon Castle

**Area:** Mallerstang, near Kirkby Stephen. **Reference type:** Ruined frontier keep; legendary ancestry.

**Real-world reference:** A castle ruin beside the young Eden, surrounded by uplands. Legend associates it with Uther Pendragon, but there is no evidence of a castle there before the twelfth century. [Source: Yorkshire Dales National Park, Pendragon Castle](https://www.yorkshiredales.org.uk/places/pendragon_castle/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A frontier keep with a grander reputation than its surviving fabric warrants. Claimants invoke an ancient dynasty while exploration reveals repeated rebuilding and contradictory evidence. It provides a useful distinction between what local people believe and what a ruin actually demonstrates.

<a id="loc-20"></a>
### LOC-20 — Devil's Bridge

**Area:** Kirkby Lonsdale. **Reference type:** Medieval crossing; trade control.

**Real-world reference:** A medieval bridge over the Lune with a long documented history of maintenance and repair. A crossing can be an important historical location in its own right. [Source: Yorkshire Dales National Park, Devil's Bridge on the River Lune](https://www.yorkshiredales.org.uk/devils-bridge-on-the-river-lune/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A strategic crossing between market town and upland road. A toll dispute, broken agreement or invented supernatural bargain motivates the encounter. Controlling or reopening the crossing can matter more than defeating its occupiers, especially when the party travels with pack animals or a wagon.

## Western and southern Lake District

<a id="loc-21"></a>
### LOC-21 — Cathedral Quarry

**Area:** Little Langdale. **Reference type:** Excavated slate chamber; massive support pillar.

**Real-world reference:** A disused slate quarry containing a dramatic chamber supported by a great rock pillar. This is an industrial excavation, not a naturally formed limestone cavern. [Source: National Trust, visiting Cathedral Quarry](https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/lake-district/the-langdales/a-guide-to-visiting-cathedral-quarry).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** The strongest single visual reference for a great worked chamber. Pillar, angled walls and a daylight opening suggest a dwarven extraction hall later reused as shrine, refuge or muster point. Its forms should visibly distinguish mined spaces from natural caves.

<a id="loc-22"></a>
### LOC-22 — Hodge Close Quarry

**Area:** Near Coniston and Tilberthwaite. **Reference type:** Flooded excavation; vertical access; spoil.

**Real-world reference:** A steep-sided former slate working with a flooded quarry pit and substantial surrounding industrial spoil. [Source: Lake District National Park, High Tilberthwaite to Little Langdale route](https://lakedistrict.gov.uk/route/high-tilberthwaite-to-little-langdale-ford-and-slater-bridge/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A drowned excavation with entrances at several heights. Players see their destination across the water long before they find a route. Pumping, lowering a boat, repairing a hoist and locating an upper passage offer different solutions. Water level becomes meaningful geography rather than decoration.

<a id="loc-23"></a>
### LOC-23 — Coppermines Valley

**Area:** Coniston. **Reference type:** Working mine infrastructure; water power; pack transport.

**Real-world reference:** Waterwheels powered equipment including pumps and ore hammers, while packhorses carried processed ore away. The landscape links mountains, minerals, water and transport. [Source: Lake District National Park, introducing Coppermines Valley](https://lakedistrict.gov.uk/route/introducing-coppermines-valley/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** An active or recently abandoned mining district. Water rights, missing pack animals, disrupted shipments and disabled pumps become quests. Combined with Gunnerside's landscape and Cathedral Quarry's interiors, it makes the Azure Mines economically and physically credible.

<a id="loc-24"></a>
### LOC-24 — Wastwater and the Screes

**Area:** Wasdale. **Reference type:** Glacial lake; volcanic scree; severe mountain frontier.

**Real-world reference:** A glacial lake below extensive slopes of loose volcanic rock. Scree continues underwater, creating a dramatic meeting of steep slope and deep lake. [Source: Geological Society, Wastwater rock-cycle reference](https://www.geolsoc.org.uk/ks3/gsl/education/resources/rockcycle/page3715.html).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A harsher frontier than the starting valleys. A rockfall blocks the lakeside path, leaving a boat journey, high pass or expensive detour. The dark rock and open water visually contrast with pale limestone and pastoral Dales scenery.

<a id="loc-25"></a>
### LOC-25 — Hardknott Roman Fort and pass

**Area:** Eskdale. **Reference type:** Remote road fort; ordered imperial architecture.

**Real-world reference:** A fort guarding a mountain route, with identifiable headquarters, commandant's accommodation, bath-house remains and a parade ground. Parts of the road it guarded remain traceable. [Source: English Heritage, Hardknott Roman Fort](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/hardknott-roman-fort).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** An old imperial outpost rather than a medieval castle. The orderly plan distinguishes its builders from later cultures. A stranded garrison, broken supply chain or surviving road marker connects old ruins to present travel. Pair with [FOL-03, a frontier guardian](northern-folklore-and-old-powers.md#fol-03), without deciding that Erith's real historical empires match Rome.

<a id="loc-26"></a>
### LOC-26 — Swinside Stone Circle

**Area:** Near Millom, south-western Lake District. **Reference type:** Prehistoric monument; rural ritual place.

**Real-world reference:** An unusually complete stone circle, also called Sunkenkirk, embedded in a rural landscape. [Source: Lake District National Park, Swinside Stone Circle](https://lakedistrict.gov.uk/explore/things-to-do/historical-places/stone-circles-in-the-lake-district/swinside-stone-circle/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A ritual place that does not announce itself through glowing runes. Local stories, weather and nearby landmarks gradually reveal its significance. It can function first as a place of negotiation, remembrance or seasonal gathering. Long Meg's separate folklore, [FOL-15](northern-folklore-and-old-powers.md#fol-15), offers a possible adaptation motif, not an assertion that both sites share the same history.

## Cumbrian coast and Morecambe Bay

<a id="loc-27"></a>
### LOC-27 — Furness Abbey

**Area:** Near Barrow-in-Furness. **Reference type:** Large red-sandstone monastic complex.

**Real-world reference:** Extensive red-sandstone ruins in a wooded valley, including substantial church, chapter-house and cloister remains. [Source: English Heritage, Furness Abbey](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/furness-abbey/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** The former headquarters of a wealthy religious order involved in land, shipping and mineral production. Red stone gives the coast a separate architectural identity. Pilgrims, refugees, officials and less welcome inhabitants reuse different wings. Its commercial and political role can be as important as its religious one.

<a id="loc-28"></a>
### LOC-28 — Piel Castle

**Area:** Piel Island, near Barrow-in-Furness. **Reference type:** Island fortress; ecclesiastical military power.

**Real-world reference:** An island fortress built by the Abbot of Furness, with a large keep, inner and outer baileys and towered curtain walls. The historical abbey–fortress relationship is valuable inspiration. [Source: English Heritage, Piel Castle](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/piel-castle/).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A harbour stronghold, isolated prison or contested island holding. Boats, provisions, weather and control of the landing become central. Pairing it with a Furness-inspired estate makes a religious power's military and commercial interests tangible. It does not automatically replace Old Rookhold, which has its own established identity.

<a id="loc-29"></a>
### LOC-29 — St Bees Head

**Area:** West Cumbrian coast. **Reference type:** Red-sandstone sea cliffs; seabird colonies; exposure.

**Real-world reference:** Red-sandstone cliffs, rocky shores and cliff-nesting seabirds form a strong contrast to limestone and slate inland. [Source: National Trust, exploring the Whitehaven coast](https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/lake-district/whitehaven-coast/exploring-the-whitehaven-coast).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A coastal watch route with an abandoned beacon, nesting ledges and wreckage below. Bird behaviour becomes environmental evidence: sudden disturbance, an abandoned colony or unnatural silence gives warning before a threat is visible. These are proposed game clues, not guaranteed signs of danger in real birdwatching.

<a id="loc-30"></a>
### LOC-30 — Morecambe Bay sands

**Area:** Lancashire–Cumbria coast. **Reference type:** Intertidal crossing; shifting channels; guided travel.

**Real-world reference:** The bay was historically crossed to avoid the long landward journey. Shifting channels, quicksand and rapidly moving tides made knowledgeable guides essential to safe passage. [Source: Lancaster City Council Museums, crossing the sands](https://www.lancaster.gov.uk/sites/museums/explore-online/local-history/crossing-the-sands).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A preparation-led journey: hire a guide, wait for the crossing window, protect baggage and judge whether a diversion is affordable. A ruin revealed between tides creates a natural deadline. Do not frame the game adaptation as practical advice for attempting the real crossing.

<a id="loc-31"></a>
### LOC-31 — Arnside and Silverdale

**Area:** Morecambe Bay fringe. **Reference type:** Coastal limestone; woodland; transition landscape.

**Real-world reference:** A compact landscape of limestone grassland, woodland, rocky headlands and estuary views. Eaves Wood includes limestone pavement beneath woodland. [Source: National Trust, Arnside and Silverdale](https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/lake-district/arnside-and-silverdale).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** The transition where familiar limestone country reaches the sea. Wooded hollows, sheltered clearings and coastal overlooks suit smugglers, small communities and secluded shrines. It supplies a quieter region between the large coastal set pieces.

<a id="loc-32"></a>
### LOC-32 — Muncaster Castle

**Area:** Near Ravenglass and Eskdale. **Reference type:** Living ancestral residence; household politics.

**Real-world reference:** A castle associated with a continuing family residence and the western Lake District landscape. Its inhabited character contrasts with the many ruins in this collection. [Source: Muncaster Castle, heritage experiences](https://www.muncaster.co.uk/heritageexperiences).

**Azure Mines possibilities:** A diplomatic location where the party bargains for access, learns coastal politics or investigates a disappearance while servants and guests watch. Use the social life of a noble household rather than making every castle abandoned. Names, ownership and any connection to existing nobles remain undecided.

## Recommended combinations

These are the original discussion's recommendations, preserved for later author review rather than entered into the implementation backlog.

| Potential use | References | Why they fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing goblin hideout | LOC-04 Yordas Cave + LOC-01 Gordale Scar | Underground chamber, stream and waterfall relationships; dramatic approach and gradual reveal. |
| Wider Azure Mines district | LOC-13 Gunnerside + LOC-14 Old Gang + LOC-23 Coppermines Valley + LOC-21 Cathedral Quarry | Scarred valley, surface settlement, functioning infrastructure and great worked interiors. |
| First-region travel | LOC-11 Buttertubs + LOC-09 Strid + LOC-20 Devil's Bridge | Pack-route choices, dangerous crossings and strategically important infrastructure. |
| Political and historical landmarks | LOC-15 Bolton Castle + LOC-19 Pendragon Castle | An inhabited power centre and a ruined frontier holding with disputed history. |
| Later coastal material | LOC-28 Piel Castle + LOC-30 Morecambe Bay + LOC-27 Furness Abbey | Boats, tides, guides and links between religious, commercial and military power. |

### Design principle worth retaining

**Borrow the relationship between landscape and human activity, not just the silhouette.** A mine needs water, fuel and transport. A stronghold needs a reason to control a valley. A bridge changes travel. An abandoned settlement leaves social and economic traces, not only a convenient dungeon entrance.

Keep pale limestone caves, worked slate chambers, volcanic mountain slopes and coastal sandstone visually distinct. Inspiration can be combined without claiming that all those rock types or industrial processes belong in the same small valley.

### Canon and implementation guardrails

The [world bible](../world-bible.md) remains authoritative for Seabrook, Harrowford, the Old Trade Road, the high-moor tower, Old Rookhold, Vale Manor and the goblin hideout. Their identities must not be merged merely because several real-world sites offer useful references. The mine's undisclosed deeper story is not explained or replaced by any research idea here. No exact map positions, distances, new rulers, named NPCs or supernatural truths are approved by this document.

Before adopting an entry, choose the fictional location and purpose, reconcile existing canon, decide what players can discover, then record the approved adaptation separately. Keep the LOC identifier so later work can trace its inspiration.
