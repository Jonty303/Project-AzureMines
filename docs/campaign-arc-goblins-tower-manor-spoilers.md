# Campaign Arc — The Paid Raiders, Moor Tower and Harrowford Manor

**AUTHOR-ONLY SPOILERS — CAMPAIGN DESIGN, NOT IMPLEMENTED GAMEPLAY.**

This establishes the causal thread the setting author has now specified. It is part of the [campaign story bible](campaign-story-bible-spoilers.md), but is deliberately kept separate from the player-facing opening, current adventure JSON, dialogue and map. The society's name, true objective and the thing found deep underground remain undefined.

## Author-established facts — canonical direction

1. The **goblin lord controlling the raiders** receives payment from the mysterious secret organisation to **disrupt trade on the Old Trade Road**. The goblins are not merely freebooters, although individual goblins may not know who finances the raids. The bugbear overlord is currently unnamed; the displaced former goblin chief is also not named yet.
2. During a previous caravan attack, the raiders captured **a dwarven trader** and **a mage travelling with the caravan**. Both were alive when separated, and neither returned to civilisation. This explains why the earlier merchant's reports listed *no returned survivors*—it does not imply everyone was killed.
3. The organisation **took the dwarf to an old tower on the high moor**, where he is **being tortured for information about the dwarven mines**. The dwarf's identity, what he truly knows and whether he is able to cooperate remain open.
4. The mage is **alive, malnourished and chained in the goblin hideout**, without his spellbook. He is distressed about its loss. A rescue should involve releasing the chains, food, water and safe recovery—not instant combat readiness.
5. The **mage's spellbook travelled with the dwarf to the moor tower**. It is a named, unique quest object with provenance. Do not leave a duplicate copy in the hideout or randomly award it as loot.
6. Returning the spellbook to the mage is personally important. Once safe and reunited with it, he **may choose to settle in Harrowford and offer magical services**. This is a conditional service unlock, not an automatic free spellcaster or a guarantee that he joins the party.
7. **Evidence in the old tower points to Lord Osric Vale of Harrowford being involved.** His involvement is established by the author; the exact degree—payments, facilitation, knowledge, coercion, self-interest or society membership—remains for later development. This should open political options to **unseat him, expose him, constrain him or bring him into line**, not require one forced boss fight.
8. The paid raids, tower interrogation and lord's involvement form a tangible **early-to-mid adventure conspiracy trail**. They do not yet explain exactly what the dwarves uncovered, the organisation's final plan or every missing caravan.

## Goblin hideout — canonical limestone-cave design

The setting author has established the full room geography, inhabitants and command structure in [Goblin Hideout — Wooded Valley and Limestone Cave](goblin-hideout-limestone-valley.md). **The apparent goblin lord is really a bugbear overlord installed by the society** to suppress trade on the Old Trade Road. He abuses his goblin subordinates and **will not bargain, trade, form an alliance or willingly surrender the captive mage**. Any earlier speculation that the overlord himself might negotiate has been superseded.

The hideout entrance sits in the rocky side of a wooded valley. **Two goblin watchers in trees** intend to ambush strangers or sound the alarm; a party that detects them first can ambush or evade them. A **long main passage** leads toward an **underground waterfall** with **three goblin guards**. Branches serve a **rubbish tip**, **guardroom (1 goblin and a large wolf)**, **shared kitchen/eating/sleeping area (six goblins including the displaced former goblin chief)**, and the **bugbear's chamber (bugbear, two goblin guards, another wolf, and the mage chained in a corner)**. A **private tunnel from the bugbear chamber connects to the waterfall**.

The **rubbish tip** has an open **hole in the floor** used for dumping most of the waste. Its destination is **deliberately unknown for future use**. A small original **Midden Grub** scavenges among the refuse; it should be a low-risk, avoidable encounter for level-one adventurers, not a trap that silently forces descent or instant death. Proposed statistics and choices are in the hideout design document.

- **Diplomacy:** The **former goblin chief**, displaced by the abusive bugbear and living in the common area, may ally with the party **against the bugbear** when given sufficient reason and security. Negotiation is with the goblins, not the unreceptive overlord.
- **Security:** Hesk's fate and whether the tree watchers raise an alarm can alter defenders' readiness. These state changes remain separate from goblins' trust and willingness to talk; not every goblin shares the same motivation.
- **Combat/stealth:** The total cave roster is 14 goblins, 2 wolves, 1 bugbear, one small scavenger, plus a captive. These are **location populations**, not a fair single encounter. The party should have room for scouting, careful tactics, withdrawal or encouraging an uprising.
- **Mage rescue:** The mage is living, chained, malnourished and afraid in the overlord's cave. After being freed he needs care and time to recover. His spellbook remains with the captured dwarf at the high-moor tower; returning it is a later separate objective.
- **Nonlinear exploration:** The waterfall/lord passage enables alternative positioning, and the refuse shaft remains a mystery to mark for a later return. The discovered map, event log and NPC registry must not spoil unseen chambers or unbuilt places.

The bugbear's **eventual outcome**—killed, captured, fled, driven out or remaining in power—and the **former chief's subsequent rule** are independent persistent consequences. Hesk's family and personal treatment remain relevant. No society-leader identity or lower-level content is established by this dungeon design.


## High-moor tower — two intertwined rescue objectives

The old tower is a new discoverable authored location, separate from **Old Rookhold**, the ruined coastal keep in Seabrook. It is on the **high moor in the Dales**, not in Harrowford or the coastal city. Its original builders and former purpose are not established.

**Captured dwarf:** He is being questioned under torture about the mines. His plight is urgent but should not become an arbitrary unwinnable timer. If introducing deadlines, provide clues, fair warnings and alternate resolutions; do not make an invisible countdown instantly kill him. Rescue is a goal independent of recovering any papers.

**Missing spellbook:** The mage's identifiable book was taken to the tower with the dwarf. The book might have been seized as evidence, as a possible source of knowledge or simply as part of confiscated possessions; **its exact significance to the organisation is not established**. It is a quest object, not random spell loot. Finding it should set a durable ownership/recovery flag and permit return to its original owner; no duplicates or repeat rewards.

**The lord clue:** The tower must contain **credible, inspectable evidence** implicating Lord Osric Vale, such as a sealed payment record, correspondence, a personal signet, an authenticated witness or consistent records. Pick the exact evidence later and let the party assess reliability. A single obscure check must not be the only route to proving his involvement. The discoverable evidence can establish a connection without disclosing the full society.

**Dwarven knowledge:** The trader may know mineral shipments, mine access rights or who arranged his capture; he is not automatically privy to the dwarven containment effort. His statements should align with what he plausibly witnessed.

## Harrowford — a political resolution, not an obligatory assassination

Once the party uncovers convincing evidence, the situation should alter dialogues at the guild, market, manor, shrine and farms. Local law, alliances, witnesses and Lord Vale's retainers may matter. Proposed choices include:

- **Confront him privately:** leverage evidence to force an end to his interference, concessions or meaningful restitution. This might leave a compromised lord in office and generate later complications.
- **Expose him publicly or to the guild:** establish facts, gather support, and push for investigation, removal or replacement. A lawful process and a power vacuum can each have consequences.
- **Unseat him by other means:** a risky option that may involve force or a political challenge, but not a default mandatory combat sequence.
- **Bargain or collude:** a morally compromising route which could gain access or resources while harming others, with long-term reputational effects.
- **Fail or defer:** the party can leave the political struggle unresolved and pursue other leads; the world reacts.

Do not automatically identify Vale as the secret society's leader. **He is involved**, but his exact responsibility and knowledge remain unassigned.

## Recurring relationships and potential services

The **mage without his book** should have a lasting personal arc. If rescued but the book remains missing, he may stay in Harrowford as a recovering witness while asking the party to find it. If the book is restored, his gratitude may make him willing to settle and provide magical services, subject to fair fees, resources and his own choice. Examples for later implementation: identifying or studying magical objects, ritual consultation, scribing appropriate wizard spells and historical research. Actual spell availability, prices and 2024 SRD rules need a separate mechanical specification; **do not promise every spell or free unlimited casting**.

The **dwarf trader** may later become a witness, trade contact, quest giver or source of mining records, with realistic fears and loyalties. He may not trust adventurers immediately after captivity.

## Optional long-term outcome — the mage claims the high-moor tower

**New possibility proposed by the setting author:** after the mage is rescued and reunited with his spellbook, he might choose to **take over the old high-moor tower** rather than settle permanently in Harrowford. This is a *branch*, not a foregone conclusion or a reward that triggers automatically.

The tower first needs to be made safe. A rescued mage who has suffered captivity and malnutrition should have time to recover, decide whether he wants the responsibility, and judge whether the party has made the place secure. Clearing the tower does not necessarily transfer legal ownership: **Lord Osric Vale's involvement** could create a dispute over land rights, leases or the tower's use. Evidence against Vale or a negotiated settlement might help the mage establish a legitimate claim.

That creates several plausible outcomes:

- **Harrowford resident:** The mage stays in town and offers a modest selection of magical services from a rented room or workshop.
- **Tower keeper:** With his book restored and the site secured, he occupies the tower as a private study, observatory, magical workshop or safe refuge. He remains a service contact the party can revisit, and might periodically visit the Harrowford market.
- **Shared project:** The party helps restore the building and perhaps contributes funds, tools or rare materials. Hollis's Yard could provide carpentry and repairs, creating a purposeful gold sink. The mage operates the tower independently; any party use of its facilities is negotiated rather than assumed.
- **Declines or leaves:** He may be unwilling, unwell, distrustful of the party or fearful of returning to the place where his spellbook was held. His future should remain possible without requiring the tower branch.

The tower can acquire **new authored scene variants and map annotations** as it changes: first occupied by captors; then cleared but neglected; then partially repaired with signs of magical study; later perhaps inhabited, protected and visited by townsfolk or adventurers. A friendly mage living there could offer identification, research, scrollwork within our rules, or clues about the mines—subject to resources and knowledge, not an unlimited free spell shop. The tower must remain **separate from Old Rookhold**, Seabrook's ruined coastal keep.

Owning a secluded magical site could be politically uncomfortable. Vale might demand rent or restitution, rivals might want the property, the society could attempt to recover abandoned evidence, and Harrowford might benefit from a resident expert while fearing what he studies. The mage's actions should change local reactions without automatically converting him into a quest dispenser or party retainer.

**Possible future flags:** `mage_rescued`, `spellbook_returned`, `mage_recovered`, `tower_cleared`, `tower_claim_resolved`, `mage_home: town | tower | elsewhere`, `tower_repairs_stage`, `tower_services_available`, `tower_party_access`. These are design labels only, not implemented state.

**Acceptance criterion:** returning the spellbook permits—rather than guarantees—the mage's decision to occupy the tower; the location changes on the map and on revisits, with consequences, service availability and any property disputes persisting after reload.

## Playability acceptance criteria for a later milestone

1. The goblin hideout can be reached by different leads and revisited; Hesk's fate can modify readiness and diplomacy without overwriting the mage's custody state.
2. The imprisoned mage can be freed, cared for and spoken to without gaining an instant full-strength follower.
3. The dwarf and mage have separate locations and fates; the spellbook exists **only at the tower** until recovered, then in party quest inventory, then with its owner.
4. A clue from hideout or town leads towards the high-moor tower, without making the rescued mage's dialogue the only route.
5. The tower can be explored, the captive dwarf can be rescued and the unique spellbook recovered, independently.
6. The tower offers at least one durable, corroborable lead implicating Osric Vale without prematurely revealing the secret society's entire identity.
7. Recovering and returning the book can unlock optional magical services in Harrowford; the unlock survives save/load and is not a free unlimited resource.
8. Evidence enables multiple viable political responses to Vale. A failed Persuasion check does not force assassination as the sole route to progress.
9. The bigger mine containment crisis remains a later mystery; resolving the goblin lord does not automatically resolve all caravan disappearances.
10. All scenes and journals display only justified character knowledge—not the author-only truths in this document.

**No new gameplay was implemented by this document.** The current v0.7 opening still ends in its short aftermath panel; the reusable narrative, map, quest, rescue and town-service systems are future work.
